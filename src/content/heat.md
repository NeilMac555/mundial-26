# Heat vulnerability — Mundial 2026

## Why this matters this year more than 2022

Three things have shifted since the original draft, and they all push the heat factor harder, not softer.

**El Niño is now locked in for the tournament window.** NOAA's Climate Prediction Center moved to El Niño Watch on April 20, with summer 2026 emergence at 85% in their latest update — up from 62% three weeks earlier. AccuWeather, ECMWF and the Old Farmer's Almanac all agree: above-average temperatures across the southern and eastern US through June and July, with the heat building as the season progresses. The Northeast is flagged for a "late surge of heat and higher humidity" specifically in tournament window. Late-stage group games and round of 32 matches will be hotter than the opening fixtures, not cooler.

**Drought has expanded, not contracted.** 61% of the lower 48 is now in moderate-plus drought, up from 56% three weeks ago. 45 states are affected. Drought conditions amplify heat — dry soil radiates rather than cools, so cities away from large bodies of water feel warmer than the absolute temperature suggests. Houston, Kansas City, Arlington (enclosed but worth noting), all in the affected zone.

**Club World Cup 2025 was the dress rehearsal and it was bad.** Borussia Dortmund's substitutes watched the first half of their Cincinnati match from the locker room because the touchline was unbearable. Niko Kovac compared the pitch to a sauna. Real Madrid players were pouring water over their heads at MetLife in 90°F afternoon heat. Chelsea-PSG final was played in 85°F with a record-breaking heat dome over the East Coast. FIFPRO subsequently estimated WBGT in afternoon Atlanta, Dallas, Houston matches would breach safe thresholds. The pressure those incidents put on FIFA is the reason mandatory hydration breaks now apply to every match — but the underlying conditions are the same or worse this summer.

## The hydration break recalibration

The 22-minute and 67-minute hydration breaks change the maths. Worth being precise about what they do and don't do, because the original assessment in the file was directionally right but worth tightening.

**What the breaks fix.** They prevent the catastrophic-end-of-second-half collapse that 2022 Qatar saw before mandated breaks. They give heat-vulnerable teams a structured cooling window when they'd previously have been winging it. They standardise conditions, so a team can plan around them tactically rather than hoping for a referee discretion call.

**What they don't fix.** Three minutes of water doesn't undo 22 minutes of high-intensity football in 33°C. Humidity still affects breathing and core temperature throughout the match — Miami is the worst-case here because the air itself is the problem, not just radiant heat. And critically, the breaks themselves create a *tactical* differential, not just a physiological one. A heat-adapted side that runs the same intensity through the break can press harder coming back; a heat-vulnerable side will spend the break trying to recover rather than scheme. So the "break helps everyone equally" framing isn't quite right — it helps acclimatised teams less because they need it less.

**Net effect on betting angles.** I'd put the heat-edge attenuation at 15-20%, not 10-15%. The original file underestimated slightly. But the relative ranking of vulnerable teams is unchanged.

## Tactical patterns to watch within matches

This is the part the original file undersold. Heat doesn't just affect overall result — it shifts within-match dynamics in ways that create specific market opportunities.

**The 25-50 minute window.** Heat-vulnerable sides typically maintain intensity through the first 22 minutes, get a break, then start to fade between minutes 25 and 50 (right before the second hydration break at 67). This is the window where vulnerable sides concede on average. Live betting on goals between the 25th and 50th minute against heat-vulnerable favourites is a structural angle, not a hunch.

**The 67-90 minute window post-revision.** Pre-mandatory-breaks, this was where heat-vulnerable sides collapsed. With the second hydration break at 67, the curve flattens — they get a recovery window right when they'd historically have been broken. So the late-match goals angle is weakened compared to 2022 Qatar reads. Adjust live-bet expectations downward for late goals against vulnerable sides.

**Substitution patterns.** Heat-vulnerable managers will use all five subs and use them earlier. This is a known pattern from Club World Cup 2025. Watch for in-game markets on substitutions (where available) — over on number of subs by the 70th minute is a structural angle for vulnerable favourites.

**Pressing intensity drops first.** Goals from clean breakthrough plays against the press tend to come earlier in heat-affected matches because the press is the first system to fail under fatigue. Sides that break the press tend to score in the 30-60 minute window specifically. If you have an opponent that plays direct (Senegal, Iran, Ghana, Australia all fit this profile against heat-vulnerable European pressers), the pattern is real.

## What I would NOT bet on the heat factor

A few things people are pricing the heat into that I'd ignore:

- **Cold-climate teams to be eliminated in groups outright.** Heat is one factor among many. Norway are in Group I with France, Senegal and Iraq — they'll likely qualify second on talent regardless of heat. Bigger angles exist on goal markets and AH lines than on tournament-progression markets.
- **Mexico City altitude as a factor.** Estadio Azteca is at 2,200m. Altitude affects pacing but not in the direction of heat — it's a separate problem and only really affects Mexico's group games. Don't conflate it.
- **"Heat dome" speculation.** AccuWeather has flagged late-summer heat dome risk for the East Coast in July. The forecast windows are too imprecise to bet 6 weeks out. If a heat dome is locked in 5-7 days pre-match, that's a live trading edge, not a pre-tournament bet.
- **Hosts as heat-managed by default.** Mexico has the altitude/cool advantage at Azteca but Monterrey is brutal. Canada has Vancouver (cool) but Toronto isn't always pleasant. USA matches are wherever the schedule puts them — Pochettino's side will play hot venues too. Don't assume host advantage cancels heat exposure.

## Bottom line

The heat factor is real, the El Niño signal makes it worse than the original draft suggested, the Club World Cup 2025 evidence confirms the danger venues are genuinely dangerous, and the hydration breaks soften but do not eliminate the edges. The five specific match angles in the original file remain valid — Germany at Houston noon is the standout, Netherlands at Houston second is conditional on draw, Scotland in Miami is real, Kansas City evenings less so post-hydration-breaks.

The structural reads (cold-team second-half fade, manager pressing-system vulnerability, sub patterns, 25-50 minute window) are the parts the original file undersold and where the actual edge lives.
